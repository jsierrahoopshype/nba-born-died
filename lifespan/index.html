    What this shows

    Each line starts at age 20 with every player in the group alive and steps down as players die. The shaded band is the 95% confidence band: it widens where few players are left, and two groups whose bands overlap cannot really be told apart. The dashed lines are U.S. men born in the same years.

    These are correlations, not causes. Height tracks era (players got taller over time), position, body size and the medical care of each generation, so a gap between groups does not mean height itself shortens or lengthens a life. The "born 1930-59" view compares heights within one span of birth years.

    The U.S. figures are a rough benchmark: NBA players are a different mix of backgrounds, health and income than U.S. men as a whole, so the gap is a description, not a measure of what playing in the NBA does to a life.

    How this works

    Who is counted. Every NBA and BAA player with a known birth date. 4 players with status unknown are not in the curves; the tables show how many of each group that is.

    Kaplan-Meier survival. Each player enters at the age of his NBA debut (never before 20) and stays in the calculation until he dies or, if living, until his current age, then drops out without counting as a death. At every age the curve multiplies in the share of players still under observation who survive that year.

    Why not average the age at death? A plain average only counts players who have already died. For recent eras and for tall players (mostly recent), the only deaths so far are the early ones, so their average looks alarmingly low. Kaplan-Meier also counts each living player as a survivor up to his current age.

    Small groups. Curves stop where fewer than 10 players remain at risk; groups under 30 players are merged with a neighbour. "n/a" in the table: too few players have reached that age to estimate it.

    The U.S. comparison. The Social Security Administration's cohort life tables for men (2024 Trustees Report, intermediate assumptions). Each NBA group is matched year by year: at every age, the U.S. death rate is averaged over the birth years of the players still under observation at that age. Both curves start at age 20.

    Confidence band. The 95% confidence band comes from Greenwood's variance on the log-log scale.
